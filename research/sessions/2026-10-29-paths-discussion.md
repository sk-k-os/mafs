## Coordinator proposal for skeptical discussion

A precise interface for full Sha finiteness is to construct independently a nonzero integer M(E) annihilating Sha(E/Q). Finiteness of the M Selmer group would then imply finiteness of Sha=Sha[M]. This is an interface to the missing theorem, not a new mechanism: with finite Selmer groups, existence of such an M is equivalent to finiteness itself. It becomes useful only if a geometric, cohomological or Euler system construction supplies M independently of the desired BSD formula or presumed Sha order.

Testing any finite collection of primes does not supply such an integer. Even finiteness at every individual prime does not imply finite support. The skeptical referee proposed the torsion group direct sum over primes of (Z/pZ)^2 as a stress test: every primary component is finite and has a nondegenerate alternating pairing, yet the total group is infinite. Pairings and square orders alone therefore do not bridge the global gap. This example is a logical obstruction to an inference, not a claimed example of an infinite Sha of an elliptic curve.
