# A theorem hypothesis separates the control from the pilot

Date: October 28, 2026. Status: submitted mathematical argument with exact finite computations. Separate assessment is recorded in the [referee report](../reviews/run-001-referee.md) when available. This is an application of standard facts, not a novelty claim, a formal proof receipt or a resolution of BSD.

## Exact claims

Let C over Q be `y^2 + y = x^3 - x`, and let B over Q be `y^2 + y = x^3 - x^2 - 10*x - 20`. The canonical identities are the coefficient vectors `[0,0,1,-1,0]` and `[0,-1,1,-10,-20]` respectively.

Claim A: the Galois representation on C[5] is irreducible over F5.

Claim B: for every nonzero rational d, the Galois representation on the usual quadratic twist B^(d)[5] is reducible over F5. The trivial square class is included.

Consequence: the irreducibility hypothesis in the selected Jetchev, Skinner and Wan prime part theorem can hold for the control at 5, but fails for every quadratic twist in the proposed pilot family at 5. This excludes this theorem application at this prime. It does not refute BSD or exclude other theorems, primes or uses of the family.

## Argument for Claim A

The exact [experiment](../experiments/run-001-local-counts/README.md) gives discriminant 37 for C. In particular 3 is a good prime distinct from 5. Over F3, every x in `{0,1,2}` satisfies `x^3-x=0`, and `y^2+y=0` has precisely the roots 0 and 2. Thus C has six affine points and one point at infinity; `a_3 = 3+1-7 = -3`.

At a good prime q distinct from 5, the characteristic polynomial of arithmetic Frobenius acting on C[5] is `T^2-a_q*T+q` modulo 5. The source for this bridge is Andrew Sutherland, MIT 18.783, Fall 2025, Lecture 25, section 25.3, pages 3 and 4, especially the trace and determinant identities on page 4. These pages fix the representation convention; the definition of irreducibility is in section 25.4 on printed page 5. [Lecture notes](https://math.mit.edu/classes/18.783/2025/LectureNotes25.pdf)

For q=3 this polynomial is `T^2+3*T+3`; its discriminant is `9-12 = -3`, or 2 in F5. The squares in F5 are 0, 1 and 4, so the polynomial has no F5 root. If C[5] had a Galois invariant F5 line, every Galois element, including this Frobenius element, would have an eigenvalue in F5 from its action on that line. This contradicts the polynomial calculation. This proves Claim A relative to the stated standard Frobenius fact.

## Argument for Claim B

The point P=(5,5) lies on B. The usual Weierstrass addition law gives `2P=(16,-61)` and `3P=(16,60)=-2P`, hence `5P=O`. For transparency, the tangent at P has slope 5 and intercept -20. The secant through P and 2P has slope -6 and intercept 35. Substitution into the curve, followed by reflection `(x,y) -> (x,-y-1)`, gives the two multiples. P is not O and 5 is prime, so P has exact order 5. Its span is a Galois invariant line in B[5].

Quadratic twisting multiplies the action on the odd torsion module by the associated quadratic character. The published source is Cremona and Freitas, *Global methods for the symplectic type of congruences between elliptic curves*, Revista Matematica Iberoamericana 38 (2022), pages 1 to 32, Lemma 2.2(2), equation (2.5), printed page 8. [Published paper](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/38965)

This can also be seen directly: put B in a short Weierstrass model `Y^2=X^3+A*X+B0` over Q, and define its twist by `Y^2=X^3+d^2*A*X+d^3*B0`. Over Q(sqrt(d)), the map to B is `(X,Y) -> (X/d,Y/(d*sqrt(d)))`. Conjugating sqrt(d) changes this map by negation. Transporting the torsion action through this map therefore multiplies each original action matrix by the scalar character value in `{1,-1}`. Multiplication by a nonzero scalar preserves every invariant line. The line generated by P consequently yields an invariant line in each twist's 5 torsion representation. The line need not have a rational generator on the twist. This proves Claim B; it does not assert rational 5 torsion on every twist.
