# Scientific practice

## The central objective

Find useful structure, then establish its consequences rigorously. The user's screenshot emphasizes that provability is vastly larger than mathematical significance. Accordingly, maintain two graphs. The proof graph records exact logical dependencies. The research map records analogies, conjectural bridges, examples, obstructions, and questions. Research map edges never become proof dependencies merely because they look promising.

Mathematical productivity is a change in justified understanding: a proved relevant lemma, a rigorously excluded route, a simpler equivalent formulation, a useful new definition, a reproducible phenomenon with clear limitations, or a sharper obstruction. Count publications, tokens, agents, and completed tasks only as operational quantities.

## Thinking modes

| Mode | Operation | Required artifact | Failure test |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Structural | Identify the invariant or obstruction controlling the desired conclusion | Exact candidate definition and bridge to the target | Definition is circular, vacuous, or merely renames the target |
| Constructive | Build examples and explicit objects | Reproducible construction with hypotheses | Construction fails outside an unnoticed special case |
| Analogical | Transfer an idea from another setting | Dictionary of objects, operations, and mismatches | The decisive property fails under the proposed correspondence |
| Adversarial | Search for the weakest step and small counterexamples | Minimal failing case or precise objection | Criticism is rhetorical and does not locate a mathematical defect |
| Experimental | Detect patterns through exact or controlled numerical computation | Dataset identity, code, bounds, precision, and reproducibility receipt | Finite evidence is promoted to a universal claim |
| Formal | Translate a stable statement and close proof obligations | Pinned source, elaborated statement, proof dependencies, checker evidence | Formal statement silently weakens the intended claim |
| Expository | Reconstruct the argument in a simpler language | Short logical skeleton and explicit dependency list | Simplification deletes a hypothesis or unresolved step |
| Opportunistic | Revisit a rejected idea after a changed premise or new theorem | Exact changed condition and a new decisive test | The same failed approach repeats with different wording |

Modes are distinct assignments, not theatrical personas. A role prompt cannot manufacture expertise or cognitive independence. Rotate modes and compare actual outcomes. A promising connection may originate in an intentionally loose analogy; every claim admitted into a proof must still satisfy logic.

## Research cycle

1. Reconstruct the strongest relevant known result from its actual source. Record its exact hypotheses and what it does not prove. Look for recent developments before calling an idea novel.
2. Express the target as a dependency graph of obligations. Mark which are known, conditional, computable, formalized, disputed, or open. Do not invent a percentage of the Millennium problem solved.
3. Locate a bottleneck whose resolution would change the strategy. Work backward from the conclusion and forward from explicit examples. A long list of easy lemmas does not by itself justify a route.
4. Propose several competing mechanisms before committing. Give each a strongest argument, a likely obstruction, and a discriminating test. Keep at least one alternative that challenges the dominant formulation when the budget permits.
5. Run the cheapest informative tests first. Counterexamples and failed hypotheses can save more effort than an additional successful example. Register expected outcomes before inspecting results when practical.
6. Develop the strongest surviving branch. Allow uninterrupted work on a coherent argument; do not interrupt every proof step with orchestration overhead. Store structured progress at natural boundaries.
7. Invite a fresh referee to reconstruct the argument independently. Then expose disagreements. The integrator resolves each objection with a proof, a corrected statement, or an explicit open obligation.
8. Formalize stable interfaces and decisive lemmas where the required library exists. If it does not, estimate the formalization debt and preserve a human mathematical review path with a weaker assurance label.
9. Present the result and remaining obstruction to the director when discussion would help. Record his original response, changed priorities, suggested sources, new hypotheses, and the next finite experiment. A human suggestion is a research input until justified mathematically.

## Learning from Perelman

Borrow the practice of developing new quantities and local arguments that expose an obstruction, then integrating them into a global proof. Read the actual papers and their later verification. Do not claim to reproduce Perelman's mind, personality, or success probability. Ricci flow methods are not automatically transferable to BSD; any proposed connection must identify a mathematically meaningful correspondence and the missing bridge.

The useful lesson is not solitary genius by roleplay. It is sustained attention to the actual geometry or arithmetic, selective invention, command of prior results, and an argument that survives reconstruction by other experts. See the sourced discussion in research-target.md.

## Relevance and novelty

For every candidate, record the exact target obligation it could advance, the implication still missing, known neighboring results, the simplest test that could kill it, and the expected information gained. The human director assesses significance. Machine rankings may help order experiments, but must display the evidence and uncertainty behind the ordering.

A novelty search returns searched sources, date, queries, nearby results, and remaining coverage gaps. It cannot certify that no one has ever proved a statement. Treat public benchmark success as calibration evidence with possible training contamination; commission fresh domain problems and proof perturbations for meaningful transfer tests.

## Protected exploration

Proposed initial allocation: 60 percent of research effort to the selected obstruction, 25 percent to reconstruction, tooling, and formalization, and 15 percent to speculative connections. Independent verification is budgeted separately and cannot be raided to make discovery numbers look better. These are planning defaults, not measured optimal values.

An exploratory idea may be wrong and still valuable if its test teaches something. Preserve why it failed. Require a changed hypothesis, new source, or new experiment before revisiting it. Repeatedly restating a conjecture is not exploration.

## Human mathematical direction

The director is the human research director. Bring him consequential questions, useful anomalies, competing routes, and a clear account of what has stalled. Use the conversation to spark new definitions, analogies and tests. Do not replace his direction with an invented expert or block routine research awaiting another director. Independent qualified mathematicians remain appropriate referees for final proof assessment; that is a separate responsibility.

Each review packet should fit a short first page: exact question; strongest current result; unresolved step; three most relevant evidence references; two competing next moves; requested decision. Attach the full proof and experiment logs. Prefer a precise ten minute expert question over an unstructured transcript dump.

Routine exploration inside accepted scope continues between reviews. Hold only work that depends on an unanswered material decision. Prepare publication or expert correspondence drafts when useful; do not send them without authorization.
